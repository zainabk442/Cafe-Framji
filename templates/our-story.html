Since 1962

Three generations on Bhau Daji Road

A wood-fired oven, four marble tables, and a family that never saw a reason to change either. Here's how Café Framji got from there to here.

62
1962 — The founding

Framji Irani sets up shop

Framji arrived in Bombay with his brothers, like many Irani families before him, and took a lease on a corner plot in Fort. He brought four marble-top tables, a set of bentwood chairs bought secondhand from a closing bakery, and a wood-fired oven he'd have shipped in from Yazd. The oven is still here. So is one of the original chairs.

78
1978 — The mirrors go up

A small renovation, mostly cosmetic

Business was steady enough by the late seventies that Framji could afford to do what every Irani café owner eventually does: put mirrors on every wall. Not for vanity — regulars say it made the narrow room feel twice as big, and gave the waiters a way to see the whole floor without turning around.

95
1995 — The second generation

Framji's son, Rustom, takes the counter

Rustom grew up doing homework at table two and took over full-time in the mid-nineties. He added the all-day breakfast menu — mostly because he was tired of customers asking for eggs at 4pm — and refused, on principle, every suggestion to introduce a espresso machine.

15
2015 — The third generation

Rustom's son steps in

The current owner grew up assuming he'd do something else entirely, studied accountancy, and came back anyway. His one concession to modern running: a booking form, so regulars can hold a table on Sunday mornings instead of standing in the queue outside. Everything else stayed exactly as it was.

26
Today

Same tables, same oven, same rule about outside food

Sixty-two years on, the chai is still poured cutting-style if you ask, the bun maska still comes from the same oven Framji shipped in, and somebody at the next table is, statistically, arguing about cricket right now.

What we won't change

The house rules

No outside food

Framji's rule from day one. It's on a plaque by the door and nobody's ever successfully argued their way around it.

Cash and common sense

We take cards now, but the till is the same one Rustom used. Some habits are just hard to retire.

Sit as long as you like

No turning tables here. A pot of chai and a newspaper have always been a valid reason to stay till closing.
